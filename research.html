Our science

Research

Questions raised in the clinic become trials, and samples from those trials go back to the laboratory. Our work is organised in two areas: clinical research and translational research.

1

Clinical Research

We design and run investigator-initiated trials that can lead to practice-changing results, and take part in a broad range of international studies that give patients access to new treatments.

As of November 2025 the team has conducted 22 investigator-initiated and 70 sponsor-initiated trials in ovarian, endometrial and cervical cancer.

Area 01From clinical questions to practice-changing trials
01

Investigator-initiated trials

Studies designed in-house from questions raised in daily practice, from single-arm phase II studies to randomized multicenter trials.

02

International collaboration

The team leads global investigator-initiated trials such as DOVE (APGOT-OV7 / ENGOT-ov80) in recurrent clear cell carcinoma.

03

Sponsor-initiated trials

More than 70 industry-sponsored studies, from phase I to phase III, bring new treatment options to patients with advanced or recurrent disease.

04

Surgery with systemic therapy

Trials of surgical strategies, including HIPEC at interval cytoreductive surgery (KGOG 3042) and secondary cytoreduction after progression on a PARP inhibitor (SOCCER-P).

2

Translational Research

Translational research bridges basic science and clinical application. We study tumor tissue and blood from our patients to understand why a treatment works, why it stops working, and who is most likely to benefit.

Many of these studies use samples collected within our own clinical trials, so findings can feed directly into the design of the next trial.

Area 02From patient samples to mechanisms and biomarkers
01

Tumor immune microenvironment

Profiling tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes in ovarian cancer: how T cells become exhausted, how this differs by BRCA1/2 status, and how checkpoint blockade can reinvigorate them.

02

Circulating tumor DNA

Serial ctDNA testing to detect minimal residual disease, predict outcome and trace resistance to PARP inhibitors, plus vaginal swab and plasma DNA profiling in endometrial cancer.

03

Homologous recombination deficiency

A functional, transcript-based measure of HRD to predict response to PARP inhibitors and DNA-damaging agents beyond BRCA1/2 mutation status.

04

Patient-derived models

Tumor tissue from patients grown as tissueoids and combined with cancer cell-derived nanovesicles to test drug response.

05

Immune changes during treatment

Immunologic changes during PARP inhibitor maintenance and neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy, studied in samples from our own trials.

06

Predicting response in endometrial cancer

Molecular classification and mismatch repair status as predictors of response to fertility-sparing hormone therapy.
